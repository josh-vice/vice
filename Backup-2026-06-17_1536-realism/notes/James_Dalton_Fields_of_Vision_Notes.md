## DVD 1 — Core Market Profile Concepts

### The Market at Its Foundation
- The market is a **simple two-way auction process**
- Motives and incentives of buyers and sellers are virtually infinite
- The market moves until it finds a price where both sides are satisfied

### The Profile Graphic
- Uses **time** (the constant) compared against **price** (the variable)
- Employing the Profile graphic in the trading arena is analogous to **putting together a puzzle**
- As you become more proficient, you start to **visualize what the profile/puzzle will look like before the day**
- The successful trader **visualizes the developing market** rather than attempting to predict the market's outcome

### Point of Control (POC)
- Also referred to as the **"Fairest Price at Which Business is Being Conducted"**
- It is the **longest line of TPOs closest to center** of the daily range
- The POC migrates as the auction finds equilibrium
- Watch the 45° line forming — when the POC is not migrating lower, sellers are getting short in the hole (a sign of strength)
- **Key insight from Dalton email:** "Look at the POC, not migrating lower. The sellers are getting short in the hole. This is the 45° line I talk about. No outside event and we could be setting up for a rally."

### Incomplete Auctions
- Incomplete auctions are **revealed through profile shape**
- An **early high with narrow initial range** signals incomplete high
- The auction completes when price trades above the early high
- Profile shape tells you what business is still unfinished

### Profile Shapes
- The Market Profile graphic represents the **structure of the market**
- The Profile graphic allows us to **listen to the market**
- Different shapes communicate trend days, rotation days, balance, excess, etc.
- Single prints (excess) stand out from normal profile development

### Key Principle
- More relevant to pay attention to **rotation on rotation days** vs trend days
- Structure repair: poor highs and lows get corrected ("repaired") over time
- Collapsed vs Expanded structure — structure contracts and expands based on participation

## DVD 2 — Day Types & Openings

### The 5 Day Types
1. **Primary Day** — Normal bell curve, both sides participate, value forms cleanly
2. **Variation of Primary** — Similar to primary but with some skew
3. **Trend Day** — Strong directional move; two subtypes:
   - *Normal Trend Day* — continuous one-direction move
   - *Double Distribution Trend Day* — two value areas form with a gap between them
4. **Double Distribution Trend Day** — Two separate distributions; a "b" or "p" shape often signals this
5. **Balanced Day** — Market auctions both directions, ends near open; tight range

### What Makes Market Profile Unique?
- **Synthesis** — the combining of separate elements to form a coherent whole
- **Analysis** — the separation of a material whole into its constituent parts for individual study
- Market Profile does **both**: it synthesizes the day's activity into a visual structure AND allows you to analyze each component

### Value vs. Price
- **We trade VALUE, not price**
- Overlapping value areas moving higher = bullish
- Overlapping value areas moving lower = bearish
- When value and price diverge — opportunity

### Opening in Relation to Yesterday's Value & Range
**4 Opening Scenarios:**
1. **Open within yesterday's range** → smaller opportunity
2. **Open outside yesterday's range** → larger opportunity
3. **Open within yesterday's value** → smaller opportunity
4. **Open outside yesterday's value** → larger opportunity

**Key:** The biggest opportunities occur when we are **out of balance**

### The 4 Pit Session Opening Types
1. **Open Drive** — Price opens and drives immediately in one direction; strong conviction
2. **Open Test Drive** — Price opens, tests the opposite direction, then drives the original direction
3. **Open Rejection Reverse** — Price opens, trades in one direction, gets rejected with enough force to drive prices back the opposite direction
4. **Open Auction** — No conviction; price auctions in both directions; patience required

### Opening Scenarios by Location
1. **Open Inside Value** — least opportunity; market is comfortable
2. **Open Outside Value, Within Range** — moderate opportunity
3. **Open Outside Range** — maximum opportunity; market strongly out of balance

### Contextual Understanding of Openings
> *"No learning happens without understanding context"*

- Openings only make sense **in context** of where price is relative to value, prior range, and longer-term references

### Time Frames (5 Types)
1. **Scalper** — very short term, tick/minute
2. **Day Timeframe** — within the session
3. **Short Term** — multi-day
4. **Intermediate Term** — weeks to months
5. **Long Term** — months to years

- **Timeframe behavior reveals itself as the session unfolds**
- This enables the trader to discern **who is dominating** the market
- And subsequently, **what strategy and tactics to utilize**

## DVD 3 — Reference Points, Balance & Excess

### The Diffusion Model (Applied to Markets)
Dalton applies the technology adoption diffusion model to market participants:
1. **Innovators** — first movers; establish the new direction
2. **Early Adopters** — follow the innovators
3. **Early Majority** — trend starts to look "obvious"
4. **Late Majority** — most people pile in
5. **Laggards** — last ones in; often signal the end of the move

**Key insight:** No matter the commodity or market being auctioned, this illustration of behavior **consistently plays out in virtually every auction**

### Reference Points (11 Key References)
1. Prior session high and low
2. Overnight high and low
3. Close — Unchanged
4. **POC** — the "Fairest Price to Do Business"
5. Rally high or pullback low
6. Bracket highs and lows
7. Spikes — base and top/bottom
8. Current session open
9. **Half back** — floating as session develops
10. Double distribution ranges
11. Longer-term references from daily, weekly, and monthly charts

### The Two Most Important Concepts: Balance & Excess

#### BALANCE
- Approximately **85% of trading days** are balancing days
- **"Balance is paradise for day traders"**
- Short-term balance = tight range, lots of rotational opportunity
- Where balance occurs provides indication of the **age of the trend**
- Balance at the top of a long move = trend aging
- Balance in the middle of a move = consolidation, likely to continue

#### EXCESS
- Excess **marks the end of one auction and the beginning of another**
- Excess starts the **trade location process all over again**
- Forms as: gaps, spikes, single prints, poor highs/lows
- Gaps are **also a form of excess**
- **Excess occurs in ALL timeframes** — daily, weekly, monthly
- Difficult to execute against excess unless you are **prepared for it** — requires trading contrary to the market's most recent activity

### Balance + Excess Together
- **Monthly balance** with excess = greater odds that new (likely innovator) sellers/buyers have entered
- After excess forms, think about **destination** — where is the market likely to go?
- "Once there is excess, we should begin to think destination"
- **Gap becomes destination** — prior gap areas become targets

### Trade Location
- **Trade location is the best tool available for risk control**
- Many traders can't expand their imagination to understand there is **more risk paying up** than getting good trade location
- After excess forms: trade location process starts over; you have a new edge, a new high or low to reference

### Dollar / Regression to Mean
- The dollar had become increasingly volatile
- In an environment where **regression to the mean** in the day timeframe is financially challenging
- Balance + Excess create the framework for understanding when mean reversion is likely vs. when momentum will continue

## DVD 4 — Volume, Preparation & Putting It All Together

### Field of Vision — The Complete Picture
> **"When you put this all together"**

Three-part framework:
- **Price advertises opportunity**
- **Time regulates the opportunity**
- **Volume measures the success or failure of the advertised opportunity**

### Volume — Contextual Use
- Volume **must be used contextually** — it does not mean the same thing in all situations
- **Volume in balancing areas** — not nearly as important; just confirms rotation
- **Volume in breakouts from balance** — extremely relevant; confirms whether new participants have entered

### New Business vs. Old Business Volume
- **New business volume** = fresh participants entering; confirms the move
- **Old business volume** = existing participants covering/exiting; does NOT confirm move
- Lack of elongation + POC not migrating in direction of price = old business (weak move)

### NYSE Volume as Reference
- Dalton used NYSE total volume plotted hourly in Excel as a reference
- Tracks cumulative volume by 30-minute period across multiple days
- **Lower volume** days vs **higher volume** days tell you about participation level and conviction

### Top-Down Perspective
Always analyze from the top down:
1. **Monthly** — Is the long-term trend up, down, or balanced?
2. **Weekly** — Where is intermediate-term value?
3. **Daily** — What is day timeframe context?

> *"In a trending market, where you are in the trend matters"*

### What Timeframe Is Controlling?
- Use reference levels from multiple timeframes to determine who is dominating
- **Intermediate term reference** levels often contain or drive the day session
- **Inside Day** — when today's range is within yesterday's range; signals balance, potential coil
- Trends exist **within other trends**: Long Term > Intermediate > Short Term > Day Timeframe

### Trading Range Identification
- Identify trading range extremes
- Excess marks the extremes of the range
- Trade within the range vs. fade breakouts based on context

### Preparing to Trade
> *"Virtually all performance literature recognizes the link between performance and preparation. Without preparation we are unlikely to overcome our emotions when it really matters."*

- Create a **Narrative** each day: the recap of all your work + identifying potential trade scenarios
- Come to the market with a plan, not a blank tablet

## DVD 4 — Live Example Walkthrough: Week of April 9–16

This section applies all concepts to a real week in the S&P and Bonds.

### Friday, April 9th
- Low was made in the **second period** — attempted direction down
- **Poor low** formed (no excess, no tail)
- Late Friday: sellers didn't appear; momentum traders pushed prices back up
- **No elongation** — long-term sellers had limited interest
- Takeaway: poor low signals unfinished business to the downside

### Monday, April 12th
- Value is **higher** — bullish sign
- However: **no elongation**, wide POC
- No real evidence of longer-term buyers
- Sellers remain. Poor low not corrected
- Dalton quote: *"I was told by a hedge client, 'Everyone has sworn off shorting. Can a break be far behind?'"* — Diffusion model at work (laggards)

### Tuesday, April 13th
- **Structure repair** attempted
- Poor low still not corrected — **carry forward buying tail**
- POC reference maintained
- Market trying to build upside but structure weak

### Wednesday, April 14th
- **Anomalies** present (jagged edge)
- Can't close the gap — **perfect day timeframe buy** setup
- Overnight trade creates key reference
- Overnight low takes out anomaly — also at the **½ back** level

### Thursday, April 15th
- Opens **at the spike base** — important reference
- Market built higher highs but failure to elongate
- Quiet selling; market running on fumes
- POC only 2 ticks below center of range
- **Almost perfectly balanced day**
- Poor low forming again

### Friday, April 16th — 1st Half
- Opens **out of balance**
- Poor highs can indicate a market that has gotten too long
- Traders continue what works until it doesn't work anymore
- Buying breaks but not getting much
- Think **Diffusion Model** — laggard buyers, innovator sellers entering
- **'b' formation** developing (buying tail, then selling pressure)

### Friday, April 16th — 2nd Half
- **'b' formation** confirmed
- **Buying tail** present — important structural reference
- Liquidity event unfolds

### April 16th Context — Big Picture
- Structure played a role in **setting up** the move AND **containing** it
- Usually on a big break, **3 structural references** will contain the break
- The poor underlying structure coming into Friday had **increased the odds against the longs**
- However: *"It did not predict the break"*
- Key insight: *"Very often we see a day timeframe struggling to trade higher; structure weakens and volume declines; however, price doesn't always decline — but the ODDS have changed"*
