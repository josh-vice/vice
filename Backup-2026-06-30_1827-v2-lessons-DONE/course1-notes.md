## Module 1: Supply & Demand

### Video 2: Understanding Price Action (Course1Module1S1)

**Definition:**
- **Price Action (PA)** = the building block of price; illustrates the psychology of buyers and sellers
- PA helps understand: supply & demand, who is in control, where price is going and why, and ultimately support & resistance (S/R)

**Chart Types:**
- **Line chart** — only shows closing price
- **Bar chart** — shows High, Open, Low, Close (HOLC)
- **Candlestick chart** — same data as bar chart but visually clearer; used throughout this course

**Japanese Candlestick Components:**
- **Body** = Open to Close; white/green = buyers in control; black/red = sellers in control
- **Wicks (Shadows)** = Upper wick = high of session; Lower wick = low of session
- **Large body** = confidence (strong buyer/seller control)
- **Small body** = indecision (neither party dominant)
- **Long lower wick** = seller exhaustion / buyers stepping in
- **Long upper wick** = buyer exhaustion / sellers stepping in

**Single Candlestick Patterns:**
| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Spinning Top (small doji) | Indecision — no dominant party |
| Inverted Hammer | Long upper wick, small body — context-dependent (bottom of trend = bullish) |
| Shooting Star | Long upper wick, small body — top of trend = bearish |
| Hammer | Long lower wick, small body — bottom of trend = bullish |
| Hanging Man | Long lower wick, small body — top of trend = bearish |
| Marubozu | Very dominant candle — one party fully in control |
| Gravestone Doji | Long upper wick, no body — sellers in control |
| Dragonfly Doji | Long lower wick, no body — buyers in control |
| Long-legged Doji | Long upper & lower wicks — heavy indecision; wait for next candle |
| Four-price Doji | Rare; seen on very low timeframes |

**Key Takeaways:**
- Price action = market participant psychology illustrated through candlesticks
- Read the story: identify who is in control (buyer or seller) and why
- Candlestick shape, body size, and wick length are all clues

### Video 3: Components of a Market (Course1Module1S2)

**What is a Market?**
- A place where buyers and sellers meet to exchange a good or service
- Modern trading = virtual/exchange-based markets
- Asset types: equities, bonds, currencies, derivatives, commodities, cryptocurrencies

**Buyers = Demand | Sellers = Supply**
- Goal: identify the **exhaustion point** of either party
  - When do buyers run out of steam to push price higher?
  - When do sellers run out of steam to push price lower?

**Supply & Demand Zones:**
- **Demand Zone** = area where buyers are concentrated and step in to push price up
- **Supply Zone** = area where sellers are concentrated and push price down
- Supply and demand zones are **finite** — they will eventually run out of buyers/sellers
  - Running out of buyers at demand → **breakdown**
  - Running out of sellers at supply → **breakout**

**Reading a Chart:**
- As a trader: look to **buy at demand zones** and **sell/short at supply zones**
- Price "ping-pongs" between demand and supply in consolidation phases
- Long lower wicks at demand zones = buyer confirmation
- Exhaustion wicks at supply zones = seller confirmation

**Key Takeaway:**
- Supply & demand is the conversation between buyers and sellers
- Supply/demand zones are the foundation for support & resistance

### Video 4: Support and Resistance (Course1Module1S3)

**Definition:**
- **Support** = area of excess demand; buyers willing to buy at certain price points → pushes price UP
- **Resistance** = area of excess supply; sellers willing to sell at certain price points → pushes price DOWN
- **Golden Rule:** Be a BUYER at support and a SELLER at resistance

**Two Types of S/R:**

1. **Diagonal S/R (Trend Lines)**
   - Valid trend line = at least **3 touches** along the line
   - Can be drawn wick-to-wick or body-to-body — pick one and stay consistent
   - Buyers enter long at diagonal support; sellers enter short at diagonal resistance

2. **Horizontal S/R**
   - Price levels that cannot be breached
   - Defines a **range-bound market**
   - Not a fixed point — they define **zones**
   - **Rule of Fives**: S/R becomes progressively weaker with each test; by the 5th touch, the level often breaks

**S/R Flip:**
- When a resistance level is broken, it becomes support (and vice versa on breakdowns)
- S/R flips = access points / trading opportunities in the direction of the trend
- Crucial to identify in real time

**Strength of S/R:**
- Determined by the volume of buyers/sellers (related to open interest)
- More tests = weaker S/R

**Key Takeaways:**
- S/R = physical illustration of supply & demand through price action
- S/R can be diagonal or horizontal
- More retests = weaker level
- Always be buyers at support, sellers at resistance
- Watch for S/R flips as trading opportunities

## Module 2: Identifying Trends

### Video 5: Trending Markets — Uptrends (Course1Module2S1)

**Uptrend (Bull Trend):**
- Defined by: **Higher Highs (HH) + Higher Lows (HL)**
- Caused by imbalance between buyers and sellers — demand puts upward pressure on price
- Buyers defend price at higher lows
- Trend continues until a supply zone causes buyer exhaustion (pivot point)
- Higher lows often occur at previous highs → S/R flip confirmation

**Downtrend (Bear Trend):**
- Defined by: **Lower Highs (LH) + Lower Lows (LL)**
- Caused by supply overpowering demand
- Trend continues until buyer demand causes seller exhaustion

**Key Rules:**
- "The trend is your friend" — trade in the direction of the larger trend
- Trading against the trend = swimming upstream; drastically reduces success probability
- Trends are **finite** — never assume a trend continues indefinitely
- Can have a **trend within a trend** across different timeframes
- A break in a lower timeframe trend within a larger trend = **break in market structure**

**Practical Application:**
- Identify HH and HL = uptrend confirmed
- Identify LH and LL = downtrend confirmed
- No clear pattern = assume you're NOT in a trend
- Never make assumptions — they are costly

### Video 6: Range-Bound Markets (Course1Module2S2)

**Definition:**
- Price bounces between a **High point (resistance)** and a **Low point (support)** of a range
- Also called: horizontal markets, consolidation phases

**Characteristics:**
- Range High = resistance (sellers/supply)
- Range Low = support (buyers/demand)
- Multiple touches of either level confirm the range
- Horizontal S/R levels define range-bound markets

**Rules:**
- Be a **BUYER at range low** (support)
- Be a **SELLER at range high** (resistance)
- Both Range High and Range Low are **finite** (limited buyers/sellers)
- Fake-outs/deviations can occur (price briefly breaches a level then snaps back)

**What Follows a Range:**
- Consolidation → **Breakout** (buyers win, uptrend begins)
- Consolidation → **Breakdown** (sellers win, downtrend begins)
- Tighter consolidation = approaching breakout/breakdown
- "Rule of Fives" applies — 5th touch often signals a break

**Key Takeaways:**
- Range-bound = not trending; defined by horizontal S/R
- Also called: consolidation phase, horizontal market
- Typically precedes expansion (breakout) or breakdown
- Watch for fake-outs and deviations
- Advanced concepts: midpoint (Fibonacci), Rule of Fives

## Module 3: Market Structure

### Video 7: What is Market Structure? (Course1Module3S1)

**Definition:**
- **Market Structure (MS)** = framework to determine if market is in uptrend, downtrend, or ranging

**Swing Points:**
- **Swing High** = a high greater than all surrounding highs
- **Swing Low** = a low lower than all surrounding lows
- Focus on **high timeframe swing points** — they define market structure
- Swing points help identify entries, exits, and overall trend direction

**Bullish Market Structure:**
- Higher Lows (HL) + Higher Highs (HH) = uptrend
- Price respects previous highs as new support (S/R flip zones)

**Bearish Market Structure:**
- Lower Highs (LH) + Lower Lows (LL) = downtrend
- Price fails to take out previous highs, then breaks below previous lows

**Key Concepts:**
- A new swing high is created when price breaks above previous swing high
- A new swing low is created when price breaks below previous swing low
- S/R flips also occur at swing point levels in both structures

**Key Takeaways:**
- Swing points define market structure
- Bullish MS = HH + HL (uptrend)
- Bearish MS = LH + LL (downtrend)
- Next: consolidation, contraction, expansion

---

### Video 8: Identifying Market Structure (Course1Module3S2)

**Consolidation → Expansion or Contraction:**
- Range-bound (consolidation) periods **precede** expansion or contraction
- **Expansion** = bullish break (breakout) → creates HH + HL → bullish market structure
- **Contraction** = bearish break (breakdown) → creates LH + LL → bearish market structure
- Continuation can also occur: existing trend resumes after consolidation

**Expansion (Bullish):**
- Price ping-pongs between buyers/sellers in range
- Buyers/demand zone breaks down slightly (fake-out/deviation) but rebounds strongly
- Impulse up takes out supply zone highs → new swing high
- Old resistance → new support (S/R flip visible)

**Contraction (Bearish):**
- Sellers/supply zone breaks up slightly (fake-out/deviation) but price reverses
- Impulse down takes out demand zone lows → new swing low
- Old support → new resistance (S/R flip visible)

**Real-World Examples (Gold Monthly, ETH Weekly):**
- Gold monthly: clear bullish MS with consolidation → expansion → HH + HL confirmation
- Gold: also showed bearish MS example with LH + LL confirmation
- ETH weekly: bearish MS → consolidation → bearish continuation (lower highs/lows)

**Key Takeaways:**
- Consolidation = trendless; expansion = bullish; contraction = bearish
- After consolidation: expansion creates HH/HL; contraction creates LH/LL
- Both expansion and contraction can also represent continuation
- Market Structure Analysis (MSA) is only relevant to the timeframe being analyzed

## Module 4: Time Frame Analysis

### Video 9: Timeframes (Course1Module4S1)

**Time Frame Analysis (TFA):**
- **Macro (HTF)** = high timeframe: years, months, weeks, days → position & swing trading
- **Micro (LTF)** = low timeframe: hours, minutes → day trading & scalping

**Why Start with HTF (High Timeframe)?**
- Identifies overall trend direction
- Shows where key buyers/sellers are at swing points and S/R levels
- HTF levels carry more weight than LTF levels
- After identifying HTF levels, drop to LTF for entries/exits

**HTF → LTF Process:**
1. Mark out swing points and S/R zones on monthly/weekly/daily
2. Drop to 4H, 1H, 15min charts
3. Look for S/R flips, swing points, candlestick structure at HTF levels
4. Identify entries and exits

**S/R Flip (Reinforced):**
- Breakout S/R flip: resistance → support (bullish)
- Breakdown S/R flip: support → resistance (bearish)
- HTF S/R flips are highest priority confluences

**Trading Styles by Timeframe:**
| Timeframe | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Monthly/Weekly/Daily | Position Trading, Swing Trading (longer term) |
| 4H/1H | Day Trading |
| 15min/5min/1min | Scalping |

**Key Takeaways:**
- Always start with macro HTF → then drop to micro LTF
- Macro levels match up with micro levels (confluence)
- S/R flips and swing points apply at all timeframes
- Choose your trading style based on preferred timeframe

## Module 5: Risk Management

### Video 12: Risk Management (Course1Module5S1)

**Core Principle:**
> "Risk management is the single most important skill to becoming a long-term profitable trader. While technical analysis will help you make money, risk management will help you keep it."

**Position Sizing Formula:**
```
Position Size = (Portfolio Value × Risk %) ÷ Stop Loss %
```
- Example: $10,000 portfolio × 1% risk ÷ 5% stop loss = 2,000 contracts
- **Capital preservation comes before profitability** — full stop

**Risk Per Trade:**
- General rule: **risk 1–5% per trade**
- 1% risk = can take 100 trades before going broke
- Never go "all in" — one wrong trade = total loss

**Why Not All-In:**
- Even the best analysts cannot predict outcomes
- What we CAN predefine: **exactly how much we risk**
- The goal: stay in the game, live to trade another day

**Risk vs. Return:**
- **Risk** = maximum amount willing to lose if trade goes against you
- **Return** = expected amount earned if trade goes in your favor
- Think in **percentages** not dollar amounts (especially as portfolio grows)

**R Multiple:**
- **Expected R multiple** (RRR) = expected return ÷ risk
- **Actual R multiple** = actual return ÷ risk (post-trade)
- Example: Entry $10,000 | Stop $9,500 | Target $11,000 → R = 2:1 (risk $500, profit potential $1,000)
- If early exit at $10,750 → actual R = 1.5 (earned $750 on $500 risk)

**Key Takeaways:**
- Risk management = only defense against bankruptcy
- Trading without risk management = gambling
- Strong risk managers outlast better analysts
- Position sizing directly affects P&L and equity curve
- Returns are NEVER guaranteed; always predefine your risk

### Video 13: Achieving Profitability (Course1Module5S2)

**Win Rate × R Multiple = Profitability:**

Profitable traders fall into one of three categories:
1. High win rate + low R multiple
2. Low win rate + high R multiple
3. High win rate + high R multiple (best case)
4. Low win rate + low R multiple = NOT profitable

**Break-Even Formula:**
```
Required Win Rate = 1 ÷ (1 + R Multiple)
```
| R Multiple | Required Win Rate to Break Even |
|---|---|
| 0.5 | 67% |
| 1.0 | 50% |
| 2.0 | 33% |
| 3.0 | 25% |

**Key Insight:** The higher your R multiple, the lower your required win rate to be profitable. → Seek **asymmetric trade setups** where R > 1.

**Drawdowns — The Painful Truth:**
- 5% drawdown requires 5.3% gain to recover
- 20% drawdown requires 25% gain to recover
- 50% drawdown requires 100% gain to recover
- → **Limit drawdowns at all costs**

**Losing Streaks (Statistics):**
- With 50% win rate: 3% chance of 5 consecutive losses
- Losing streaks are **inevitable** — the system must carry you through
- More risk per trade = harder to recover from losing streaks

**The Power of Compounding:**
- Example: $10,000 | 55% win rate | 2x R | 2% risk/trade → exponential equity growth
- Compounding is how professional traders make a living
- **"The Holy Grail of trading does not exist"** — but a systematic, statistical approach gets you closest

**Key Takeaways:**
- Think in percentages, not dollar amounts
- Losing streaks are inevitable — trust the system
- Win rate × R multiple = profitability framework
- Compounding is powerful, but only works with a solid system
- Must journal to track win rate and R multiples over time

### Video 14: Optimizing Returns (Course1Module5S3)

**Kelly Criterion:**
- Scientific approach to betting/position sizing: how much of your portfolio to risk to maximize long-term returns
- **Simple formula:** `K = 2P - 1`
  - K = % of portfolio to risk
  - P = probability of winning
  - If K is negative → do NOT take the trade

- **Modified Kelly Criterion (for variable R multiples):**
  ```
  K = ((B × P) - Q) ÷ B
  ```
  - B = net odds (R multiple)
  - P = win rate (probability of winning)
  - Q = probability of losing (1 - P)

- Example: R multiple = 3:1, Win rate = 55% → K = 40% (extreme — needs adjustment)

**Pareto's Principle (80/20 Rule):**
- 80% of effects come from 20% of causes
- In trading: ~20% of trades produce the majority of returns (the "home runs")
- ~80% of trades are "throwaways" (normal returns within 1 standard deviation)
- Big losers also exist — newer traders emotionally over-hold losing positions

**Modified Kelly + Pareto = Optimal Risk %:**
```
Optimal Risk = Modified Kelly Output × 20%
```
- Example: 40% Kelly × 20% = **8% optimal risk**
- This accounts for the 80% of throwaway trades
- Limits risk → system survives losing streaks → compounding works over time

**Journaling:**
- The ONLY way to improve long-term
- Minimum required entries per trade:
  - Expected RRR (risk:reward ratio)
  - Actual R multiple
  - Entry, Target, Stop Loss
  - Risk %
  - Screenshot of chart setup
- Optional but recommended:
  - Emotions
  - Hours of sleep
  - Hunger levels
  - Why you took the trade
- Journaling reveals: what ties winners together, what mistakes losers share

**Key Takeaways:**
- Kelly Criterion = scientific position sizing framework
- Modified Kelly + Pareto Principle = optimal risk formula
- Journaling is mandatory for building a trading system
- Without data, you can't implement Kelly, win rate analysis, or R multiple frameworks
- "Without journaling we will never be able to implement a framework for risk management"
